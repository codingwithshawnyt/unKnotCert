The canonicalization procedure (Algorithm~\ref{alg:canonical}) remaps
crossing IDs to the consecutive integers $1,2,\dots,n$ in order of first
appearance.  A single pass over the $2n$ tokens of the Gauss word processes
each token in $O(1)$ amortized time (dictionary lookup and insertion),
giving $O(n)$ total time per call.  This is minimal: the subroutine must at
least read the input.  Every state generated during BRS is canonicalized
before insertion into the visited set, contributing $O(V \cdot n)$ to the
overall runtime, where $V$ is the number of states visited.
